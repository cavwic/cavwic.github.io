## Position

The dangerous phrase in a proposal is not "unconfirmed." The danger is writing an unconfirmed point as a fact. Once an interface version, site condition, third-party responsibility, or product specification becomes an assumption, the price, schedule, and acceptance plan inherit the same mistake.

I separate information into confirmed, open, assumed, and not applicable. Every open item needs an owner and due date. Every assumption states what changes if it fails. A not-applicable item still needs a reason. The table may look less polished than a page full of conclusions, but it lets engineering, delivery, and the customer see the same risks before making commitments.

Some people worry that visible unknowns look unprofessional. In project work, the opposite is usually true. Professional judgment does not mean knowing every answer in advance. It means knowing which answers can change the solution and choosing the cheapest reliable way to verify them.

Each unknown has three valid destinations: verify it from a formal source, ask the responsible party, or put it into a POC test. Unsupported claims do not belong in a product matrix or a customer commitment.
