## Position

Degrees of freedom describe a mechanism, not a task result. Two hands with the same DOF may have completely different actuator counts, coupling, backlash, backdrivability, and control interfaces.

An underactuated design may be simpler and more durable for enveloping grasps. Teleoperation and in-hand adjustment place more value on independent control and tactile feedback. Giving points to DOF without a task turns a design preference into a false objective ranking.

Early market screening is a valid counterexample because a short field list can narrow the candidates quickly. DOF can be one entry field, but it must sit beside actuator count, tactile sensing, size, interfaces, payload definitions, and the completeness of public documentation. Unknown fields should become vendor questions, not estimated averages.

The conclusion should state which candidate deserves which test, not which product ranks first. Differences between hands become useful only when they appear against representative objects and repeatable records.
