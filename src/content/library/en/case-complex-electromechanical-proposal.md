## Context and disclosure boundary

This case comes from my actual presales responsibilities on complex electromechanical systems. Customer names, locations, models, prices, internal interfaces, and bid documents have been removed. What remains is the method and the work I personally performed.

## Problem

The initial request focused on target capability and equipment configuration, while the site, electrical supply, access routes, operating process, upstream and downstream systems, and acceptance criteria were still unsettled. Quoting directly from a product configuration would have risked delivering usable equipment that could not run within the site's actual process.

## My work

I took part in the site survey and separated the findings into five groups: tasks and objects, space and infrastructure, people and process, hardware and software interfaces, and acceptance and service. Items that could not be confirmed on site entered an open-issues list with a named owner on the customer, third-party, or internal side.

The proposal placed equipment capability, site modifications, deployment conditions, and interface ownership in one structure. The technical bid did more than state compliance. It explained the implementation path, assumptions, deviations, and deliverables. Reviews checked whether product evidence covered the task boundary and whether any written commitment went beyond the available evidence.

## Result and reflection

The proposal moved into further technical discussion and delivery coordination. Site and interface issues were visible before implementation. Commercial outcomes are not published because those records do not belong on a personal website.

The transfer to embodied-robotics work is direct. The robot body is one part of a larger system. Site conditions, people, adjacent systems, safety, and acceptance must enter the proposal or product specifications cannot add up to a complete delivery.
